The Scale

Seven levels, one scale

Ratings run 0 to 7. Tap a level to see what it takes.

0.0–0.9Rookie

Complete beginner. Needs basic instruction to play. Typically 1–5 matches, or no racquet-sport background. Learning grip, ready position, and where to stand.

1.0–1.9Warm-Up

Can hit normal forehands and backhands. Some racquet-sport background, typically 5–10 padel matches. Ball goes over, rallies are short, glass is still an obstacle rather than a tool.

2.0–2.9First ServeLeaderboard

Typically 20+ matches. Sustains rallies, understands positioning, uses the back glass.

2.0–2.5 · First Serve — rallies are survival. Returns the ball but doesn't choose where it goes. Back glass works when the bounce is friendly. Volleys are blocked, not directed.
2.6–2.9 · First Serve+ — rallies with intent. Starts constructing points, plays the back glass off both wings, controls the volley. Knows they should be at the net but can't always hold it.
3.0–4.0Break PointLeaderboard

Typically 50+ matches, a year or more of play, or inter-level competition in another racquet sport. Net play, communication, long rallies, smashes.

3.0–3.5 · Break Point — attempts the bandeja, lands it in easy situations. Holds the net when the lob is short. Calls "mine" and "yours." Backhand off the wall is functional.
3.6–4.0 · Break Point+ — bandeja is repeatable, not lucky. First víbora appears. Reads the lob early and moves back without panicking. Wins the net and keeps it for more than one shot.
4.1–4.9Match PointLeaderboard

Typically 100+ matches, two-plus years of consistent play, or a competitive background in another racquet sport. Bandeja is a genuine holding shot, used to keep the net rather than survive at it. Víbora with direction and spin, played on purpose. Chiquita used deliberately to force a weak volley. Comfortable playing out of the corner off double glass. Understands the transition zone. Wins and loses the net as a pair by design. Competitive in local tournaments.

5.0–5.9Golden Point

Competes at state or national level. Full smash arsenal — bandeja, víbora, rulo, por tres off the side wall, por cuatro attempted. Defends from the back using the glass as a weapon rather than a bailout. Reads the weaker opponent and weaker wing within a few games and targets it. Runs pre-agreed patterns with a regular partner, including serve signals. Trains under a coach. Padel-specific physical preparation. Error rate stays low under pressure.

6.0–7.0Championship

National champion, international circuit, or FIP-ranked. Complete arsenal at full pressure, with tempo as a tactical choice rather than a reaction. Periodised professional preparation. Padel is the occupation.

FAQ

Common questions

How often does it update?

After every session. Your new rating and rank appear within 24 hours of your match.

What's the difference between Score and Rating?

Score is your raw total match points. Rating turns that into your level (the decimal) — and rating is what your rank and league are based on.

Two players have the same rating — who ranks higher?

The one with the higher Score. If score is also equal, they share the same rank.

Can I move up a league?

Yes. When your rating crosses into the next band, you move up. It's based on sustained performance and reviewed by the DPC team.
